# Evaluation of Wi-Fi Radiation Effects on Antibiotic Susceptibility, Metabolic Activity and Biofilm Formation by Escherichia <i>Coli</i> 0157H7, <i>Staphylococcus Aureus</i> and <i>Staphylococcus Epidermis</i>.

*journal non renseigné — 2019*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<h4>Background</h4>The radiation emitted from electromagnetic fields (EMF) can cause biological effects on prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, including non-thermal effects.<h4>Objective</h4>The present study evaluated the non-thermal effects of wireless fidelity (Wi-Fi) operating at 2.4 GHz part of non-ionizing EMF on different pathogenic bacterial strains (<i>Escherichia coli</i> 0157H7, <i>Staphylococcus aureus, and Staphylococcus epidermis</i>). Antibiotic resistance, motility, metabolic activity and biofilm formation were examined.<h4>Material and methods</h4>In this case-control, a Wi-Fi router was used as a source of microwaves and also bacterial cells were exposed to Wi-Fi radiation continuously for 24 and 48 hours. The antibiotic susceptibility was carried out using a disc diffusion method on Müller Hinton agar plates. Motility of <i>Escherichia coli</i> 0157H7 was conducted on motility agar plates. Cell metabolic activity and biofilm formation were performed using 3-(4, 5-Dimethylthiazol-2yl)-2, 5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay and crystal violet quantification, respectively.<h4>Results</h4>The exposure to Wi-Fi radiation altered motility and antibiotic susceptibility of <i>Escherichia coli</i> 0157H7. However, there was no effect Wi-Fi radiation on antibiotic susceptibility of <i>Staphylococcus aureus and Staphylococcus epidermis</i>. On the other hand, the exposed cells, as compared to the unexposed control, showed an increased metabolic activity and biofilm formation ability in <i>Escherichia coli</i> 0157H7, <i>Staphylococcus aureus and Staphylococcus epidermis</i>.<h4>Conclusion</h4>These results proposed that Wi-Fi exposure acted on bacteria in stressful manner by increasing antibiotic resistance and motility of <i>Escherichia coli</i> 0157H7, as well as enhancing biofilm formation by <i>Escherichia coli</i> 0157H7, <i>Staphylococcus aureus and Staphylococcus epidermis</i>. The findings may have implications for the management of serious diseases caused by these infectious bacteria.
