# Timing and Energy Optimization of Dual-Energy CT for Brain Metastasis Conspicuity: A Prospective 24-Patient Study.

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<h4>Background</h4>Brain metastases are frequently encountered and typically evaluated by MRI, but MRI is not always available or feasible. Dual-energy CT (DECT) with virtual monochromatic reconstructions (VMRs) may offer a rapid, accessible alternative. We aimed to determine the optimal post-contrast timing and VMR energy for visualizing brain metastases using DECT.<h4>Materials and methods</h4>This prospective study enrolled 24 patients with known brain metastases. DECT head scans were acquired at 90 seconds, 5 minutes, 10 minutes, and 20 minutes post-contrast using a dual-source scanner. VMR images were generated from 40 to 190 keV. For each time point, we measured lesion and background attenuation across keV, and calculated signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and contrast-to-noise ratio (CNR). Friedman and Wilcoxon tests were used to compare timing and energy effects.<h4>Results</h4>Lesion conspicuity declined after the vascular phase. At 90 seconds, average CNR and SNR were 2.22 ± 0.39 (95% CI: 1.83-2.62) and 8.19 ± 0.67 (7.52-8.85), respectively. These values at 20 minutes measured 1.85 ± 0.33 and 8.34 ± 0.64. CNR peaked consistently at 40 keV, whereas SNR peaked between 70 and 100 keV depending on phase. Statistical tests confirmed significant variation across both time and energy ( P  < 0.001).<h4>Conclusions</h4>Optimal visualization of brain metastases on DECT is achieved at 90 seconds post-contrast using low-to-mid keV reconstructions. Although 40 keV maximized contrast, 70 to 85 keV offered a favorable balance of contrast and noise, supporting its use for interpretation and radiosurgical planning.
