# Virtual non-calcium dual-energy computed tomography for postoperative lumbar spine evaluation following pedicle screw instrumentation.

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<h4>Purpose</h4>To compare standard grayscale CT, 140-keV virtual monoenergetic imaging, and virtual non-calcium (VNCa) for detection of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-defined relevant foraminal stenosis, disc protrusion or extrusion, and relevant nerve root compression.<h4>Methods</h4>This retrospective study included patients who underwent dual-energy computed tomography (DECT) on a 128-slice single-tube system and MRI within 1 week between August and December 2025; CT reconstructions were read in consensus. Foraminal stenosis and nerve root compression grades 2-3 were considered imaging-defined relevant findings. Only segments with an MRI confidence score of 5 were included. Diagnostic performance and CT confidence scores were summarized descriptively.<h4>Results</h4>Twenty-three patients (mean age, 60 ± 12 years; 15 women) contributed 185 evaluated segments. MRI showed relevant foraminal stenosis in 26 segments and disc protrusion or extrusion and relevant nerve root compression in 18 segments each. Foraminal-stenosis sensitivity was 100.0% (26/26) for VNCa and 92.3% (24/26) for both comparators; specificity was 88.7%, 71.1%, and 72.3% for VNCa, standard CT, and 140-keV CT, respectively. Disc protrusion or extrusion sensitivity was 77.8% (14/18) for VNCa and 11.1% (2/18) for both comparators. Standard CT and VNCa each had 83.3% sensitivity and 97.0% specificity for nerve root compression.<h4>Conclusion</h4>In this small, selected cohort, VNCa had higher observed specificity for MRI-defined foraminal stenosis and higher observed sensitivity for disc protrusion or extrusion. These exploratory findings require confirmation in larger studies that account for patient-level clustering and include more artifact-limited examinations.<h4>Clinical significance</h4>VNCa reconstruction from an existing DECT examination may complement grayscale CT for evaluating MRI-defined foraminal stenosis and disc protrusion or extrusion following lumbar pedicle screw instrumentation without requiring additional scanning.
