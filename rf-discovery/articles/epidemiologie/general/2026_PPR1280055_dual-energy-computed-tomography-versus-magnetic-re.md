# Dual Energy Computed Tomography Versus Magnetic Resonance Imaging for Liver Fat Fraction Quantification

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>Purpose  Non-invasive quantification of hepatic steatosis is critical for the diagnosis and management of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD). While MRI chemical-shift encoding with in phase/opposed phase (IP/OP) is a reliable method for fat assessment, dual-energy CT (DECT) offers a potential opportunistic alternative using multi-material decomposition (MMD). We compared DECT MMD fat fraction with MRI IP/OP fat fraction for steatosis grading. Methods  In this retrospective cohort study of 45 patients, two ROIs (2 cm²) were placed in hepatic segments VI and VII on both DECT and MRI. Correlation (Spearman), agreement (Bland–Altman), diagnostic performance (ROC), and inter-modality reliability (ICC) were assessed. Results  DECT and MRI fat fractions correlated positively in both segments (Spearman ρ = 0.50, P < 0.001 for segment VI; ρ = 0.57, P < 0.001 for segment VII). Bland–Altman analysis revealed systematic underestimation by DECT (mean difference − 3.39% for VI; −2.57% for VII). Area under the curve for mild steatosis was 0.77 (95% CI 0.63–0.91) for both segments, improving to 0.88–0.99 for moderate-to-severe disease. Inter-modality ICC indicated moderate reliability (0.62 for VI, 0.66 for VII). Time interval between scans did not affect measurement differences (P > 0.05). Conclusions  DECT MMD demonstrates moderate-to-good diagnostic accuracy for moderate and severe hepatic steatosis but is limited in the detection of mild disease. These findings support the role of DECT as an opportunistic screening tool for clinically significant steatosis rather than a substitute for dedicated MRI-based quantification.</p>
