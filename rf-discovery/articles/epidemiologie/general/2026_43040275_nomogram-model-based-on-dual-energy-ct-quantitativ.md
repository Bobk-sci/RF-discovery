# Nomogram model based on dual-energy CT quantitative parameters and radiomics for preoperative prediction of microvascular invasion in hepatocellular carcinoma.

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<h4>Purpose</h4>To explore the potential value of a nomogram model based on dual-energy CT (DECT) quantitative parameters and radiomics for preoperative prediction of microvascular invasion (MVI) in hepatocellular carcinoma.<h4>Methods</h4>A retrospective analysis was conducted on 145 patients with hepatocellular carcinoma who underwent surgical resection. The cohort was randomly divided into a training cohort (n = 101) and a validation cohort (n = 44). Clinical and imaging (semantic imaging features and DECT quantitative parameters) data were analyzed. Intraclass correlation coefficient, Pearson correlation analysis, and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression were employed to select radiomic features from portal venous phase and virtual monoenergetic image (40 keV), which were then used to calculate the radiomics score. Logistic regression was adopted to develop clinical and radiomic models; an integrated combined model was further established and plotted as a nomogram. The performance of three models was evaluated by discrimination, calibration, and decision curves. Internal validation was further implemented using repeated cross-validation and bootstrap resampling.<h4>Results</h4>The AUC of the clinical model, radiomics model, and combined model for predicting MVI in the training cohort were 0.683 (95% CI: 0.576, 0.789), 0.886 (95% CI: 0.823, 0.948), and 0.898 (95% CI: 0.838, 0.958), and in the validation cohort were 0.702 (95% CI: 0.541, 0.863), 0.829 (95% CI: 0.702, 0.956), and 0.844 (95% CI: 0.725, 0.963). The calibration and decision curves confirmed that the fusion model was well calibrated and had clinicalutility. The mean AUC of repeated 10 × 10-fold cross-validation was 0.862 (95% CI: 0.616, 1.000), and the bias-corrected AUC from 1000-time bootstrap internal validation was 0.863 (95% CI: 0.849, 0.870).<h4>Conclusion</h4>The nomogram model based on DECT quantitative parameters and radiomics possesses certain value for the preoperative prediction of MVI.
