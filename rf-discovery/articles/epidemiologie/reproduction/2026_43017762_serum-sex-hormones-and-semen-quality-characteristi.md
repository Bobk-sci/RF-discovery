# [Serum sex hormones and semen quality characteristics in populations exposed to microwave radiation: A cross-sectional study].

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

Objective To evaluate the association between occupational exposure to microwave radiation and the characteristics of male serum sex hormones and semen quality. Methods A cross-sectional study of male reproductive function was conducted from June 2024 to April 2025 at workplaces involving potential microwave radiation exposure, including space tracking, telemetry, and command stations and high-power microwave testing facilities. Participants were classified into control and exposed groups according to whether their job involved microwave radiation exposure. All participants underwent measurements of five serum sex hormones and routine semen analysis. Results Among 137 participants, 109 were from inland regions (55 controls and 54 exposed) and 28 were from coastal regions (all exposed). In the primary analysis of the inland population, PRL was higher in the exposed group than in controls (6.44 ［5.16，10.31］ vs 5.53 ［3.88，6.89］ ng/mL; unadjusted P=0.004; Holm-adjusted P=0.019), with consistent results in the log-transformed model (geometric mean ratio=1.334，95%CI 1.106－1.610; P=0.003). No significant between-group differences were found in E2, FSH, LH, T, or any semen parameter. Exposure duration was not correlated with testosterone among inland exposed participants (Spearman's ρ=0.097, P=0.483). Full-sample sensitivity analyses yielded consistent results. Exploratory regional analyses showed higher progressive motility, total motility, and sperm concentration in coastal than in inland participants.Conclusion Occupational microwave radiation exposure is associated with higher PRL levels, although the clinical significance still needs to be interpreted with caution. The current data do not show a clear association between exposure and other sex hormones or abnormal semen quality. Exploratory comparisons among regions suggest that the semen quality in coastal areas is higher than that in inland areas.
