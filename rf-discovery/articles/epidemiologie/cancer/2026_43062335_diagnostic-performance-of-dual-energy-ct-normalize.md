# Diagnostic Performance of Dual-energy CT Normalized Iodine Concentration in the Assessment of Soft-tissue Tumor Recurrence.

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

Purpose To assess the diagnostic performance of dual-energy CT (DECT) in differentiating postoperative recurrence from scar tissue in participants with soft-tissue tumors. Materials and Methods This prospective observational study included participants with pathologically confirmed soft-tissue tumors who underwent postoperative DECT between December 2021 and September 2024. Thirty-four participants underwent concurrent apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) mapping. DECT parameters were derived from arterial and venous phase lesion images. Histopathology examination served as the reference standard. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were used to identify DECT parameters associated with postoperative recurrence. Receiver operating characteristic analysis was used to evaluate diagnostic performance and calculate the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value. The DeLong test was used to compare areas under the receiver operating characteristic curve for the selected DECT parameter and ADC among participants with both DECT and ADC data. Results Forty-eight participants were included (mean age ± SD, 42.5 years ± 18.5; 26 male). Venous phase normalized iodine concentration (NIC; odds ratio, 4.60; 95% CI: 1.37, 15.41; <i>P</i> = .01) was associated with postoperative recurrence, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.83 (95% CI: 0.71, 0.95; <i>P</i> < .001); its sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value were 70% (14 of 20), 85.7% (24 of 28), 77.8% (14 of 18), and 80% (24 of 30), respectively. ADC demonstrated an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.75 (95% CI: 0.55, 0.95; <i>P</i> = .01), with a sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value of 61.5% (8 of 13), 95.2% (20 of 21), 88.9% (8 of 9), and 80% (20 of 25), respectively. Venous phase NIC and ADC showed comparable diagnostic performance. Conclusion Venous phase NIC demonstrated good diagnostic performance in differentiating postoperative recurrence from scar tissue in participants with soft-tissue tumors, with performance comparable to ADC. <b>Keywords:</b> CT, MRI, MR-Diffusion Weighted Imaging, Soft Tissues/Skin, Dual-Energy CT, Soft Tissue Tumor, Recurrence, Apparent Diffusion Coefficient <i>Supplemental material is available for this article.</i> © RSNA, 2026.
