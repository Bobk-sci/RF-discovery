# Defect-engineered erbium iron garnet via high-pressure torsion for enhanced magnetic hyperthermia.

*RSC advances — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

High-Pressure Torsion (HPT) is a promising severe plastic deformation technique for tailoring the functional properties of magnetic materials. In this study, we investigated the impact of HPT on the magnetic and hyperthermia properties of erbium iron garnet (Er3Fe5O12) particles synthesized via a sol-gel auto-combustion method. HPT processing induced significant grain refinement, reducing particles from micron-scale (∼2.3 µm) to the submicron/nanoscale (∼170-550 nm mean size), with ultra-fine fractions as small as 40 nm. While the saturation magnetization (MS) decreased due to surface disorder, the Specific Absorption Rate (SAR) improved from 2.8 W g-1 (as-synthesized) to 4.396 W g-1 (processed at 240 MPa). This enhancement is attributed to a substantial increase in the effective magnetic anisotropy (K eff) arising from HPT-induced lattice microstrain and defect accumulation, which promote hysteresis-loss-dominated heating under an alternating magnetic field. These results demonstrate that high-pressure torsion is an effective post-synthesis strategy for activating weak ferrimagnetic garnets for magnetic fluid hyperthermia applications, offering a defect-driven pathway to expand the design space of hyperthermia materials beyond conventional iron oxides.
