# Multiphase dual-energy CT parameters combined with CT features in differentiating three common lung cancers

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>  Objective  To evaluate the differential value of triphasic contrast-enhanced dual-energy CT (DECT) parameters combined with CT features for three common lung cancers: adenocarcinoma (AC), squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), and small cell lung cancer (SCLC). Methods and materials  A total of 151 pathologically confirmed lung cancer patients were prospectively enrolled. Dual-source CT triphasic (arterial, venous, delayed) enhanced scans were performed to obtain DECT quantitative parameters (40/80keV CT value, spectral curve slope λHU, iodine concentration (IC), normalized iodine concentration (NIC)). Sixteen CT features were analyzed by two observers in a double-blind manner. Intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) were used to test inter-observer consistency. Univariate/multivariate Logistic regression screened independent predictors, and DECT, CT feature, and combined models were constructed. Diagnostic efficacy was evaluated via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, area under the curve (AUC), and Delong test. Results  Among 151 patients (81 AC, 38 SCC, 32 SCLC; mean age 64.16 ± 10.25 years; 95 males, 56 females), 13 CT features and all triphasic DECT parameters showed significant differences (all  <italic>p</italic>   < 0.05). AC was predominantly peripheral (67.90%) with pleural retraction (79.01%). SCLC was mainly central (93.75%) with mediastinal lymph node fusion (87.50%). SCLC had lower parameters than AC and SCC; IC and NIC differences between AC and SCC were more prominent in venous/delayed phases. The combined model exhibited the best efficacy (  <italic>p</italic>   < 0.05 vs. single models): AUC 0.873 (AC vs. SCC), 0.980 (AC vs. SCLC), 0.937 (SCC vs. SCLC). Conclusion  DECT parameters combined with CT features significantly improve lung cancer differentiation accuracy.  </p>
