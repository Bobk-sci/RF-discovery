# Machine Learning Prediction of Rice Seedling Growth and Physiological Responses Under Controlled Electromagnetic Field Exposure Using Early-Stage Longitudinal Features

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>Reliable prediction of plant growth and physiological characteristics can aid effective phenotyping and computational evaluation of environmental treatments. The purpose of this study was to create a leakage-controlled machine learning system to predict the endpoint characteristics of rice seedlings under controlled electromagnetic field (EMF) conditions with early longitudinal measurements. Two data sets were analyzed, one synthetic and the other generated in the computer, consisting of the 2800 observations of 200 plants randomly distributed from control and low, medium and high-EMF exposure groups respectively. The morphological, biomass, chlorophyll, leaf-area and image-derived phenotypic variables were summarized into longitudinal features for Days 3–7 and Day-14 measurements were only used as prediction endpoints. Plant-level grouped training, validation and independent testing sets with a ratio of 70:15:15 were adopted so as to reduce the amount of information leaked from repeated measurements taken from individual plants. Three models were tested for predicting final height, dry biomass and chlorophyll content: Random Forest, Bagged Ensemble and Gaussian Process Regression. The models had an R² of 0.9645, 0.9698 and 0.6676 for the synthetic data set, and 0.9051, 0.9202 and 0.8194 for the computational data set. Independent-test RMSE values ranged from 0.1783–0.00421 cm/g for the synthetic dataset and 0.3298–0.00915 cm/g and 1.1339 for the computational data based on the predicted trait. In general, early longitudinal phenotypes showed promise for computational prediction of rice seedling endpoints and model performance depended on trait and data generation structure. The framework offers a repeatable method for the prediction and comparison of leakage-controlled rice phenotypic reactions using controlled EMF exposure.</p>
