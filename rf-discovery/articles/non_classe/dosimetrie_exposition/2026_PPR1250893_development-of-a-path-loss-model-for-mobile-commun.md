# Development of a Path Loss Model for Mobile Communication Signal Propagation over Water Bodies

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>Existing wireless propagation models are optimized for terrestrial and urban environments. Still, their accuracy declines over water bodies due to unique channel characteristics such as high reflectivity, surface conductivity, and evaporation ducting. This creates uncertainty for network planners and optimizers deploying coastal, maritime, and inland water communication systems. This work conducted a measurement campaign to derive a path loss model for over water bodies’ channel environment at 1800 MHz signal propagation carrier frequency. The derived models are for the environment of over fresh water bodies and ocean environment, this is because water salinity, which is different for fresh and salt water affect signal propagation. The path loss exponent for over fresh water is 2, with the addition of model saturating signal power of -19.43dBm which yield the mean error with reference to the measured signal power value of 0.0067dB and for over salt water, the path loss exponent is 2.88, with the addition of model saturating signal power of -10.52dBm which yield the mean error with reference to the measured signal power value of 0.00143dB. While for free space and two ray models on over fresh water yield the mean errors of -19.43dB and -45.3dB respectively and for on over ocean water yield the mean errors of -13dB and -48.62dB respectively.</p>
