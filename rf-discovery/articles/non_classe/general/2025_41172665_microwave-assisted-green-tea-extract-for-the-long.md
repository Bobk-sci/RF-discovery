# Microwave-assisted green tea extract for the long-term effective immobilization of Cr(VI) and Cd(II) in contaminated soils: The crucial role of silicate-bound Fe(II).

*journal non renseigné — 2025*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

Chromium (VI) and cadmium (II) are common inorganic soil pollutants posing threats to soil and humans. This study explored a microwave (MW)-assisted stabilization of Cr(VI) and Cd(II) in co-contaminated soil with green tea extract (GTE). Results showed that microwave-assisted GTE treatment promoted silicate-bound Fe(II) formation, facilitating efficient immobilization of Cr(VI) and Cd(II). Under the given conditions (2.0 wt% GTE, 800 W microwave irradiation for 20 min), 99.9% of Cr(VI) and 98.4% of Cd(II) were effectively immobilized from the iron-rich soil. Characterization techniques such as High-Resolution Transmission Electron Microscopy (HRTEM), Scanning Electron Microscopy with Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy (SEM-EDS), X-ray Diffraction (XRD), Fourier-Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR), and X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS) demonstrated that microwave radiation promotes electron transfer, enabling the silicate-bound Fe(II) to effectively immobilize Cr(VI) and Cd(II). The stabilized Cr was eventually converted to Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> and FeCr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub> on the mineral surface. In addition, the increase of iron-containing crystalline phases in the product greatly enhances the binding of Cd(II) on its surface and in its lattice structure, thereby increasing the fixation efficiency. Moreover, after 360 days of atmospheric immobilization, Cr(VI) and Cd(II) leaching concentrations stayed below the 0.05 mg·L<sup>-1</sup> toxicity limit. This study highlights the pivotal role of silicate-bound Fe(II) in microwave-assisted remediation of heavy metal-contaminated soils and provides new insights into the effective utilization of soil iron mineral resources for promoting soil health.
