# Evaluation of Short-Term Exposure to 2.4 GHz Radiofrequency Radiation Emitted from Wi-Fi Routers on the Antimicrobial Susceptibility of <i>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</i> and <i>Staphylococcus aureus</i>.

*journal non renseigné — 2020*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<h4>Background</h4>Overuse of antibiotics is a cause of bacterial resistance. It is known that electromagnetic waves emitted from electrical devices can cause changes in biological systems. This study aimed at evaluating the effects of short-term exposure to electromagnetic fields emitted from common Wi-Fi routers on changes in antibiotic sensitivity to opportunistic pathogenic bacteria.<h4>Materials and methods</h4>Standard strains of bacteria were prepared in this study. Antibiotic susceptibility test, based on the Kirby-Bauer disk diffusion method, was carried out in Mueller-Hinton agar plates. Two different antibiotic susceptibility tests for <i>Staphylococcus aureus</i> and <i>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</i> were conducted after exposure to 2.4-GHz radiofrequency radiation. The control group was not exposed to radiation.<h4>Results</h4>Our findings revealed that by increasing the duration of exposure to electromagnetic waves at a frequency of 2.4 GHz, bacterial resistance increased against <i>S. aureus</i> and <i>P. aeruginosa</i>, especially after 24 hours (P<0.05).<h4>Conclusion</h4>The use of electromagnetic waves with a frequency of 2.4 GHz can be a suitable method for infection control and treatment.
