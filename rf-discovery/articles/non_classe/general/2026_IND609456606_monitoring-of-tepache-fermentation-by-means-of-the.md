# Monitoring of tepache fermentation by means of the dielectric permittivity at 2.45 GHz

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

Tepache has been a widely consumed fermented Mexican beverage since the pre-Hispanic era, and its consumption has been linked to benefits such as improved intestinal balance, protection against potentially pathogenic bacteria, and a reduction in the occurrence of gastrointestinal diseases. Commonly used methods for monitoring fermentation, such as hydrometers, use considerable sample volumes, and are destructive, as the case of liquid chromatography. In this work, the physicochemical characteristics of tepache (titratable acidity, pH, brix degree, alcohol by volume, glucose and moisture) are presented, as well as its dielectric properties at the frequency of 2.45GHz by the cylindrical resonant cavity method on days 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 of fermentation. Titratable acidity, pH, brix degree, alcohol by volume, glucose and the imaginary part of the permittivity did not show significant daily changes. The real part of the permittivity decreased from 83.54 to 80.41 with increasing fermentation time. These small differences in the real part of permittivity were detected because of the high-quality factor of the cavity which was of 7252.9. The penetration depth indicated that the electromagnetic wave associated with the measurement of dielectric properties completely penetrates the tepache samples, evaluating their entirety. This parameter had a range from 8.99 to 9.62mm. The information presented in this work can be used for the design and manufacture of microwave sensors that can monitor the tepache fermentation process nondestructively, in real time, and that also use smaller sample volumes than conventional methods. The dielectric properties of tepache at 2.45GHz are presented for fermentation monitoring purposes. This could be used for the design of microwave sensors.
