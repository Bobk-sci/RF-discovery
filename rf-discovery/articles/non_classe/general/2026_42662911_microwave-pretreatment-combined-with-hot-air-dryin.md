# Microwave pretreatment combined with hot air drying enhances the drying efficiency and quality properties of &lt;i&gt;Fructus Aurantii&lt;/i&gt; by altering its microstructure and moisture state distribution.

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

As a seasonal herbal medicine with high water content, fresh <i>Fructus Aurantii</i> (<i>Citrus aurantium</i> L.) cannot be efficiently dried using traditional hot-air drying (HAD) alone. This study introduces microwave pretreatment combined with HAD to investigate the effects of microwave pretreatment time (0, 2, 4, 6, and 8 min) on the drying characteristics, microstructure, moisture distribution, and physicochemical quality of <i>F. Aurantii</i> dried with hot air. Microwave pretreatment formed a micropore formation and altered water-state distribution, thereby shortening the drying time of <i>F. Aurantii</i> by 11.11% - 80.56%. Lewis, Page, and Weibull models were established to describe moisture-ratio changes of <i>F. Aurantii</i> with drying time, and all three models were able to satisfactorily predict the moisture changes during the drying process. Microwave pretreatment at 700 W for 6 min significantly enhanced the quality of <i>F. Aurantii</i>, increasing the rehydration ratio, as well as naringin and neohesperidin contents, by 12.65%, 29.17%, and 31.09%, respectively. The optimal appearance was achieved under 700 W for 4 min pretreatment. This study provides valuable insights for further optimization of microwave-assisted HAD processes for <i>F. Aurantii</i>.
