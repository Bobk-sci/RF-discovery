# Innovative Microwave Foamed Egg White-Cork Composite for Enhanced Acoustic Absorption: Valorizing By-Products into Sustainable Construction Materials

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>This study explores an innovative approach to enhancing the sound absorption properties of cork-based composites for sustainable construction applications. We developed and compared two types of composites: a conventional cork-mortar mixture and a novel composite incorporating microwave-foamed egg white-cork composite. The foamed composite was created by subjecting a mixture of cork granules and liquid egg white to 2.45 GHz microwave radiation, resulting in a highly porous structure. This foamed composite was then embedded in a mortar mix to create the final composite. Acoustic testing revealed significant improvements in sound absorption properties for the composite containing microwave-expanded foamed egg white-cork compared to the conventional cork-mortar mixture. The enhanced performance is attributed to the increased air content and complex pore structure of the foamed composite. Mechanical and morphological tests were conducted to assess the overall material properties of both composites. Additionally, fire resistance tests demonstrated favorable results for both materials, with the foamed composite maintaining good flame-retardant properties. Our findings suggest that this innovative, easy-to-produce composite offers an effective solution for noise attenuation in building applications. The use of potential waste materials (cork by-products and eggs) in the production process further enhances the sustainability profile of this material. This research contributes to the development of eco-friendly, sustainable acoustic materials that align with circular economy principles in the construction industry. The MCEWP composite achieved sound absorption coefficients of up to 0.313 at 4000 Hz and 0.286 at 1000 Hz, with an open porosity of 33.3%, compared to 0.05 and 6.3% porosity for plain mortar, while maintaining flame resistance up to ~ 400°C without ignition. An estimated NRC-like value of 0.15 was obtained for comparative purposes.</p>
