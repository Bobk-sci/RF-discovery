# Distal Occlusion Tracker Sign in the 24 Hours Dual-Energy CT control after Thrombectomy: Significance and association with clinical outcome

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>Purpose  The distal occlusion tracker (DOT) sign in the immediate post thrombectomy flat-panel detector CT (FPDCT) has been identified as a marker of persistent hypoperfusion. However, its applicability and significance in Dual-Energy CT (DECT) imaging performed 24 hours post-treatment remains unclear. We systematically investigated the DOT sign in the DECT with the aim to assess its relations with thrombectomy procedure and patients’ clinical outcome. Methods  We retrospectively analyzed data from our institutional prospective registry of patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) treated with mechanical thrombectomy between January 2020 and January 2025. Inclusion criteria were: (I) patients with AIS due to large vessel occlusion (LVO), (II) availability of DECT imaging at 24 hours post-treatment, and (III) absence of missing data. We collected location and type of the sign, patients’ baseline characteristics, data regarding mechanical thrombectomy and clinical outcomes. Association of the DOT sign with technical and clinical outcomes were evaluated using logistic regression analysis. Results  Among the patients analyzed, 28.2% (n = 111) exhibited a DOT sign on DECT at 24 hours. The presence of the DOT sign was significantly associated with lower successful (TICI≥2b67) and optimal (TICI≥2c) reperfusion rates (42.2% vs 80%, and 22% vs 60% respectively; p < 0.001), worse clinical evolution (median 24h NIHSS 18 [16–21] vs 10 [9–13], p < 0.001). The DOT sign was independently associated with a poor functional outcome (aOR = 0.44, 95%CI = 0.21–0.92, p = 0.029). Conclusion  The DOT sign observed in DECT at 24 hours post-treatment seems to be associated with lower recanalization rate, worse clinical evolution and poor functional outcome.</p>
