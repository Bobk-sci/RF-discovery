# A CubeSat-Based Laser Power Transmission Framework for Sustained Lunar Surface Operations

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>The lunar night — approximately 354 hours of complete solar darkness with surface temperatures reaching − 173°C — was formally ranked as the single highest-priority unsolved technology challenge in civil space exploration by NASA's Space Technology Mission Directorate in 2024. This paper proposes and rigorously evaluates the Orbital Solar Relay Network (OSRN): a constellation of CubeSat-class spacecraft in 100 km polar low lunar orbit equipped with deployable photovoltaic arrays and diode-pumped solid-state lasers (DPSSL, λ = 1064 nm) that transmit harvested solar energy to photovoltaic laser power converter (PVLPC) arrays on the lunar surface throughout the lunar night. Corrected beam optics analysis — using the Gaussian beam propagation model — confirms that laser WPT is uniquely suited to the LLO-to-surface geometry. A 10 cm transmitter aperture at 1064 nm produces a beam waist of 67.7 cm at 100 km range and 135.5 cm at 200 km, yielding beam collection efficiencies of 99.6% and 75.0% respectively onto a 2×2 m PVLPC receiver. This stands in contrast to microwave transmission at 2.45 GHz (λ = 12.24 cm), which produces a half-angle beam divergence of 28.5° from a 0.3 m aperture — yielding a spot radius of 54 km at 100 km range and rendering microwave WPT from CubeSat-scale apertures physically infeasible at LLO ranges without apertures exceeding 800 m. The critical enabling constraint for the laser architecture is fine beam pointing: the beam divergence half-angle of 6.77 µrad requires a pointing accuracy of σ ≤ 1–2 µrad, achievable with a fast steering mirror (FSM) and beacon-tracking system consistent with LLCD-heritage technology (TRL 7). The end-to-end link budget yields 40.2 W delivered DC per 6U satellite (80.5 W for 12U) at 200 km range with σ = 1 µrad pointing, for an end-to-end efficiency of 5.9%. An 18-satellite Walker Star constellation with simultaneous transmission from all satellites delivers approximately 1.45 kW to a fixed 2×2 m surface PVLPC array — sufficient for early robotic and thermal management operations. Orbital coverage analysis confirms greater than 97% power availability at the lunar south pole for this constellation. These results are quantitatively bounded: the architecture as described delivers 100 W (3 × 6U sats), 500 W (6 × 12U sats), and 1.45 kW (18 × 12U sats). Scaling beyond this range requires additional satellites or receiver aperture expansion, both of which are characterized in the sensitivity analysis. The primary technology development requirement is a space-qualified CubeSat-scale DPSSL with FSM pointing, currently at TRL 3–4.</p>
