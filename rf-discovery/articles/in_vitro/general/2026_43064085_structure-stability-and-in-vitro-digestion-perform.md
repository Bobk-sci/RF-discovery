# Structure, stability and in vitro digestion performance of whey protein-inulin glycosylation conjugates and anthocyanins complexes by microwave-assisted wet-heating based on Maillard reaction.

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

To address the instability and low bioavailability of anthocyanins (ANC), this study developed a protein-polysaccharide delivery system based on whey protein (WP)-inulin (INU) conjugates. Microwave-assisted glycosylation, rather than traditional wet-heating, was employed to construct a porous network architecture within the conjugates to optimize ligand encapsulation and controlled release. Structural characterization revealed that microwave irradiation induced protein unfolding and exposed reactive sites. This process significantly increased grafting efficiency and minimized browning compared to conventional methods. Multi-spectroscopic analysis, combined with molecular docking and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, elucidated the interaction mechanism. ANC was effectively encapsulated within the hydrophobic pockets of the conjugates primarily through hydrogen bonding, π-π stacking, and hydrophobic interactions, exhibiting high binding affinity via a static quenching mechanism. The steric hindrance provided by the covalently grafted inulin chains effectively shielded ANC from thermal degradation, alkaline pH, and metal ions. In vitro simulated digestion demonstrated that the glycosylated carrier significantly enhanced ANC resistance to gastric proteolysis and facilitated targeted release in the intestinal phase. This study provides insights into designing porous functional food carriers for the efficient delivery of labile bioactive compounds.
