# Impact of in vitro exposure to 5G-modulated 3.5 GHz fields on human forebrain organoids

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>The development of 5G mobile technology exposes populations to electromagnetic fields at 3.5 GHz, raising concerns about their effects on human brain development. Most studies on the cerebral effects of radiofrequencies have used rodents, despite major differences between human and rodent corticogenesis, including progenitor subtypes and maturation timing. Here, 40-day-old human forebrain organoids, with actively proliferating apical and basal neural progenitors, were exposed to 5G-modulated 3.5 GHz radiofrequencies at an average specific absorption rate (SAR) of 1.5 W/kg. Effects on neural and glial development were assessed at 7 and 13 weeks using immunocytochemistry and RNA sequencing. Exposed and control organoids showed comparable size and morphology. Progenitor subtype identity and number, proliferation, and apoptotic death rates were similar at both stages. No significant differences were observed in the proportions of cortical neurons, oligodendrocytes, or astrocytes. Transcriptomic analysis confirmed neuronal maturation from 7 to 13 weeks but revealed no significant change in gene expression following exposure. These findings suggest that, under the in vitro conditions tested, 5G-modulated 3.5 GHz radiofrequencies have no major effect on human forebrain organoid development.</p>
