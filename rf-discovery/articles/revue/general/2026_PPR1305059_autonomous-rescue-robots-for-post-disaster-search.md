# Autonomous Rescue Robots for Post-Disaster Search, Navigation, and Victim Assistance

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

Natural and man-made disasters such as earthquakes, building collapses, floods, and industrial accidents create environments that are too dangerous, unstable, or inaccessible for human first responders to enter safely, yet the first hours after such events are the most critical for locating survivors. Autonomous rescue robots offer a way to extend the reach of search-and-rescue (SAR) teams into rubble, collapsed structures, and contaminated zones while reducing risk to human personnel. This paper reviews the state of the art in autonomous rescue robotics, organized around three core capabilities: search and perception, navigation in GPS-denied and cluttered environments, and victim detection and assistance. We examine platform design choices (wheeled, tracked, legged, aerial, and hybrid systems), localization and mapping strategies such as LiDAR- and vision-based SLAM, multimodal victim-detection sensing (thermal imaging, gas sensing, acoustic and ultra-wideband methods), and the human-robot interaction challenges that arise when robots operate alongside responders and survivors. Representative systems and deployments, including CENTAURO, UAV swarm search efforts, and field robots used at Fukushima, are discussed as case studies. We conclude by identifying open challenges in autonomy, robustness, communication, and standardization, and outline directions for future research, including multi-robot coordination and the integration of learning-based perception.
