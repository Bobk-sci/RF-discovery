# The Effects of Electromagnetic Field Exposure From Static to Intermediate Frequencies (0 to 100 kHz) on Fertility: A Protocol For A Systematic Review On Experimental Studies.

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

Exposure to manmade static, extremely low frequency, and intermediate frequency electric and magnetic fields is widespread and is expected to change due to expanding power infrastructures and wireless power applications. Although conclusive evidence of adverse health effects of these exposures is lacking, experimental studies suggest potential effects on male and female fertility, as well as pregnancy and birth outcomes. To support risk assessment and policy decisions, this protocol describes proposed methods for the systematic review that will evaluate the published data on effects of exposure to electric, magnetic, and electromagnetic fields with frequencies from 0 to 100 kilohertz. The review will focus on three key areas: integrity and function of the male reproductive system, integrity and function of the female reproductive system, and in utero exposure effects on pregnancy outcomes, congenital disorders, and delayed effects. Both in vivo and in vitro studies involving humans, rodents, and Lagomorpha will be included. Studies must include comparisons between exposed groups and groups with no or sham exposure, without restrictions on language or publication date. Databases to be searched include PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, and the EMF-Portal. Where studies are sufficiently similar, data will be combined in meta-analyses; otherwise, findings will be reported narratively. The risk of bias in studies will be evaluated using a specialized rating tool designed for human and animal research. Certainty of conclusions regarding associations between exposure and fertility outcomes will be assessed using an established grading system, rating evidence as high, moderate, low, or very low. This review aims to provide a transparent synthesis to clarify potential reproductive risks linked to low-frequency electromagnetic field exposure.
