# A Biophysical Perspective on Microwave-Induced Neurobiological Effects.

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

Microwave radiation interacts with the nervous system through a cascade of electromagnetic, thermal, and mechanical perturbations that drive a broad range of neurobiological responses. Although exposure guidelines are based on specific absorption rate (SAR), substantive evidence indicates that neurobiological effects arise across frequencies, waveforms, and SAR levels that SAR alone cannot characterize. We distinguish net-thermal, transient-thermal, and non-thermal mechanisms according to whether a response is mediated by the net temperature rise, by the heating rate and temperature gradients, or largerly independent of temperature change. This review traces the physical perturbation cascade from electromagnetic field distribution through thermal dynamics to thermoelastic stress, and reviews five biological endpoints: genotoxicity, molecular dysregulation and signaling, blood-brain barrier (BBB) permeability, electrophysiology, and behavioral changes. A consistent finding is that many studies report associations with only SAR and frequency, omitting the intermediate physical quantities that drive the responses, and thus obscuring a mechanistic understanding of the causal chain. Mapping of reported studies into the SAR-frequency space reveals a clustering within the 0.9-3 GHz band and near SAR = 2 W/kg, with biological alterations reported below these limits under both continuous and pulsed exposures. These findings motivate future work that directly quantifies spatiotemporal physical perturbations to close the gap between exposure conditions and neurobiological outcomes.
