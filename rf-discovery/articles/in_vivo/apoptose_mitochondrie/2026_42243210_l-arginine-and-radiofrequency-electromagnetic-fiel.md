# L arginine and radiofrequency electromagnetic field attenuate inflammatory and apoptotic injury after experimental subarachnoid hemorrhage.

*Scientific reports — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) triggers intense neuroinflammatory and apoptotic cascades that contribute to secondary brain injury, delayed cerebral ischemia, and poor neurological outcomes. Inflammatory mediators such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) and signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) amplify oxidative stress and endothelial dysfunction, whereas suppression of the hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α)/endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS)/vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) pathway impairs hypoxic adaptation and neuronal survival. L-arginine (L-ARG), a nitric oxide precursor, and radiofrequency electromagnetic field (RF-EMF) therapy each exhibit neuroprotective potential through anti-inflammatory and microcirculatory mechanisms. Adult male Wistar rats were assigned to Control, SAH, SAH + L-ARG, SAH + RF, and SAH + L-ARG + RF groups (n = 8 per group). SAH was induced by autologous arterial blood injection into the cisterna magna, followed by daily treatment with L-ARG (500 mg/kg, p.o.) and/or RF-EMF (27.12 MHz, 30 min/day) for seven days. SAH markedly increased NF-κB, TNF-α, Cas-3, IL-6, and STAT3 expression while suppressing HIF-1α, eNOS, and VEGF transcripts. Combined treatment was associated with the most pronounced improvements across measured molecular markers, including reduced inflammation and apoptosis and normalization of mitochondrial regulators (↓BAX, ↑BCL-2). These findings suggest that combined L-ARG and RF-EMF exposure is associated with attenuation of inflammation and apoptosis-related molecular injury after experimental SAH; however, functional and perfusion-based outcomes are required before therapeutic efficacy can be inferred.
