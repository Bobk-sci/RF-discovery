# Modulatory effects of alpha-lipoic acid on cardiac tissue alterations associated with 2.45 GHz radiofrequency electromagnetic radiation exposure.

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

The widespread use of devices emitting electromagnetic radiation (EMR) at 2.45 GHz has raised concerns regarding their potential impact on biological systems, particularly the cardiovascular system. This study aimed to characterize cardiac tissue alterations associated with repeated 2.45 GHz EMR exposure and to evaluate whether ALA administration modulated these alterations. EMR exposure was generated using a voltage-controlled oscillator and a low-noise RF amplifier, and the electric-field intensity within the exposure cage was measured using a calibrated electromagnetic field meter. The EMR and ALA + EMR groups were exposed to 2.45 GHz EMR at 2 h per day for 30 days. The ALA and ALA + EMR groups received ALA (30 mg/kg/day) by oral gavage for 30 consecutive days; in the ALA + EMR group, ALA was administered 1 h before each RF-EMR exposure session. Histopathological and histomorphometric evaluations revealed significant lymphocytic infiltration, collagen deposition, and increased mast cell area fraction in the EMR group compared to controls. These alterations were markedly alleviated in the ALA + EMR group. Furthermore, 2.45 GHz EMR exposure significantly increased the immunoreactivity of TNF-α, NF-κB, IL-6, iNOS, and S100A4, as well as serum cTnI and CK levels. ALA administration significantly attenuated these alterations. Stereological analysis further showed increased myocardial volume density in the EMR group, which ALA + EMR significantly attenuated. Collectively, these findings indicate that ALA administration attenuated cardiac structural, inflammatory, and biochemical alterations observed under the present 2.45 GHz RF-EMR exposure conditions; however, the experimental design does not establish a causal RF-EMR-specific effect.
