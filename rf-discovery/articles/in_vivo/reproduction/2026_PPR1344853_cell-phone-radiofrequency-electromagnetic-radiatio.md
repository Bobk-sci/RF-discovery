# Cell phone radiofrequency electromagnetic radiation exposure alters mouse sperm quality and impairs preimplantation development of embryos derived from exposed sperm

*journal non renseigné — 2026*

## Résumé (texte d'origine)

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>  Cell phone radiofrequency electromagnetic radiation (RF-EMR) may adversely affect male reproductive function, but its effects on sperm and embryos derived from exposed sperm remain incompletely understood. This study investigated the effects of 30-min in vitro exposure to RF-EMR (900–1800 MHz, call mode; SAR ~ 0.6–1.8 W/kg; 10 cm distance) on mouse sperm quality and subsequent preimplantation embryo development. Sperm count, motility, viability, morphology, DNA fragmentation, acrosome reaction (AR), malondialdehyde (MDA) levels, and mitochondrial gene expression (  <italic>mt-Nd1, mt-Nd2</italic>  , and  <italic>mt-Atp6</italic>  ) were assessed. Embryos generated by in vitro fertilization (IVF) using exposed sperm were monitored by time-lapse imaging. RF-EMR exposure significantly reduced progressive motility, viability, and normal morphology (P < 0.001), while sperm count and AR were unaffected. DNA fragmentation significantly increased following exposure (P < 0.0001), accompanied by elevated MDA levels (P = 0.0491) and downregulation of all assessed mitochondrial genes (P < 0.05). Embryos derived from exposed sperm exhibited delayed cleavage and compaction, reduced embryo quality and survival, and increased fragmentation and developmental arrest compared with controls (P < 0.05). These findings suggest that short-duration exposure of mouse sperm to cell phone RF-EMR can impair sperm functional and molecular characteristics and is associated with adverse preimplantation development of embryos derived from exposed sperm.  </p>
